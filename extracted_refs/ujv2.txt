1 INTRODUCTION 
The purpose of this document is to describe the technical solution for the Signalling system of Hanoi Pilot 
Light Metro Line 3 (HNL3) based on URBALIS™ / U400 Communication Based Train Control (CBTC) 
solution and in compliance with contract. 
1.1 URBALIS™ / U400 (CBTC) KEY POINTS & MAIN FEATURES 
The URBALIS™ / U400 (CBTC) solution is Alstom’s advanced solution for mass transit CBTC application. 
The Signalling system is a radio -based CBTC based on a proven moving block principle. The solution was 
developed to be able to perform all Automatic Train Control, Interlocking, and Mai ntenance functions r e-
quired for automatic mass transit rail operation with Train Drivers.  The URBALIS™ / U400 (CBTC) mo v-
ing block principles allow achieving the contractual performances in particular in terms of headway, journey 
time, RAMS and stopping accuracy. 
Also, its advanced and modular architecture allows achieving other important performance goals namely 
regarding scalability. 
The Signalling solution offers major advantages. Below is a non-exhaustive list of main key points: 
 A proven in use CBTC solution, in revenue service for 10 years on complex metro applications, 
 A solution with Automatic Train Control (ATC) and Interlocking hosted in separate applications and 
platforms, to allow interlocking operation, in case of ATC failure, 
 A radio independent from CBTC application, radio handover is managed only by the radio, in order 
to have a radio as “off-the-shelf” as possible, 
 A redundant solution to avoid single point of failure, 
 A URBALIS™ / U400 (CBTC) solution offering secondary train detection (ST D), in order to allow 
management of non-communicating trains (in degraded mode), 
 A system designed for a working life of at least 30 years, 
 Facilities to remotely detect alarms and diagnostic failures, 
 A strong IT security implementation to prevent deliber ate or accidental intrusion of external people 
as hackers, 
 A scalable and modular architecture that allows interlocking operation by sectors, in case of commu-
nication failure.

2 SIGNALLING SYSTEM ARCHITECTURES 
2.1 GENERAL SIGNALLING SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE 
2.1.1 General description 
The main subsystems of the CBTC Signalling System URBALIS™ / U400 (CBTC) system are:  
 Automatic Train Control (ATC) composed by:   
o Carborne Controller (CC), in the trains 
o Zone Controller (ZC), 
o Line Controller (LC), 
 Interlocking (IXL), a computer-based interlocking (CBI) composed by: 
o Central Interlocking (one dedicated Central Interlocking for main line, one dedicated Central 
Interlocking for Depot) 
o IO modules, based on the Alstom Smart IO product,  
 Front End Processor (FEP), 
 Maintenance Support System (MSS) for maintenance management composed by: 
o MSS servers,  
o MSS workstation, 
 Data Communication System (DCS) which is made of: 
o Backbone Transmission Network (BTN), that is divided into  redundant networks (red & 
blue) for CBTC communication, 
o Radio Transmission System through the free propagation medium (Radio Access Point 
(RAP) on trackside and radio on-board the trains): radio redundant networks (red& blue) for 
CBTC communication, 
o Network Management system (NMS). 
The trackside ATC is composed of the Zone Controller and the Line Controller. The role of the Zone Co n-
troller is to ensure the protection of the train and to manage the movement authority with the Carborne Co n-
troller. The role of the Line Controller is to manage the Temporary Speed Restriction and the control of 
software version in the trains. 
The Data Communication System is the sub -system of the architecture which ensures the IP communication 
between all sub -systems: ATC, Interlocking and MSS. A continuous bi -directional radio communication is 
guaranteed at each time between the track and the train. 
Through the backbone and the radio, ea ch sub-system can communicate directly with another one. For e x-
ample the ATS communicates directly with the Carbone Controller for the regulation orders.

2.2 SIGNALLING SYSTEM STRUCTURE IN HNL3 
In the frame of HNL3, the proposed signalling system is made of:  
 On-board URBALIS™ / U400 (CBTC) system for each equipped train, composed of two Carborne 
Controller 
 Centralized URBALIS™ / U400 (CBTC) system for mainline, composed of: 
o Central core equipment located in main station S1 (NHON) and Backup equipment in OCC 
Building: 
 Zone Controller (ZC) [REF13], 
 Line Controller (LC) [REF13], 
 Central Interlocking (CIXL) [REF13], 
 Front End Processors (FEP) [REF20], 
 Maintenance Support System (MSS) [REF22], 
 Network Management System (NMS),  
o Distributed equipment in main stations S1 (NHON), S5 (LE DUC THO), S8 (CAU GIAY) 
and S12 (Hanoi Railway Station) to interface with trackside equipment: 
 SMART IO equipment [REF16] with associated relays [REF15] to interface with 
points, signals, axle counters and state of power traction, 
 Backbone Transmission Network (BTN) equipment [REF7] and Data Communica-
tion System (DCS) equipment [REF18] to interface with Trackside Radio equipment 
(Radio Access Points) , 
 Distributed equipment in the secondary stations S3 (PHU DIEN) and S10 (CAT 
LINH) to interface with trackside equipment: 
o Backbone Transmission Network (BTN) and Data Communication System (DCS) equip-
ment to interface with Trackside Radio equipment (Radio Access Points), 
o Equipment spread along the track : 
 DCS Radio Access Points [REF8], 
 ATC Beacons [REF12],  
 Signals [REF5],  
 Axle counters [REF17]. 
 Dedicated Interlocking system for Depot, composed of: 
o Central Interlocking (CIXL), 
o SMART IO equipment with associated relays to interface with Depot points, signals and ax-
le counters, 
o Backbone Transmission Network (BTN) equipment. 
 All Signalling equipment is powered by a dedicated power supply system [REF21].

3 SUB-SYSTEMS DESCRIPTION 
3.1 CBI SUB-SYSTEM 
3.1.1 Presentation 
The Interlocking is an essential part in a system to control safe movements of trains within a certain area. 
The interlocking subsystem is a vital s ystem specially designed to generate safe routes for trains. For this it 
monitors train and point positions, and controls points and signals. Equipment along the track is controlled 
through remote IO modules dispatched over different equipment rooms. 
In order to generate safe train -routes, following route settings from ATS, the interlocking reads track occ u-
pancies, sets and locks points, locks routes, controls signals, informs ATC and ATS. The interlocking e x-
changes with ATC that also performs train detecti on. While a point or route is locked it cannot be used for 
other routes. While a train is running a route, the part of the route behind the train is released so other trains 
can use it. The route is released section per section as the train moves along the  route. This task requires a 
SIL4 system (according to EN50129). 
To realize this function Alstom provides the SMARTLOCK 400 interlocking system that consists of:  
 Interlocking for mainline,  
 Interlocking for depot, 
 IO modules, named Smart IO, interfaced with vital relays to track equipment, 
 A computer for logging and diagnostics purpose, named Support System,  
The communication between these components is done through the Backbone Transmission Network.

3.1.2 Interlocking Functions 
3.1.2.1 Route Control 
Routes can be controlled in several ways by the URBALIS™ / U400 (CBTC) solution. Route is defined from 
an origin signal to destination signal. 
When automatic route setting is activated in the ATS, the ATS automatically requests route setting when a 
train enters a route demand area based on train tracking information in addition to  the trip or pattern assoc i-
ated to that train. 
Routes can also be set in manual mode from the ATS.  
The URBALIS™ / U400 (CBTC) system provides the means and data: 
 To remotely perform a switch calling. 
 To remotely perform a switch blocking or unblocking  
 To remotely perform a signal blocking or unblocking 
 To perform a route setting. 
 To perform a route cancellation 
 To set and subsequently release fleet mode for route setting. 
 To perform a sub-route manual release 
These commands are available to ATS Operator from ATS Workstation. 
3.1.2.1.1 Process route interlocking 
The URBALIS™ / U400 (CBTC) CBI sub -system protects trains from front or flank collision, and from 
derailment. 
The secondary detection is used in order to detect non -talkative train and to process the correct route inter-
locking. To achieve safety objectives of the route interlocking function, the URBALIS™ / U400 (CBTC) 
system monitors the status of the secondary detection, using a combination of data from secondary detection 
and localisation of URBALIS™ / U400 (CBTC)-equipped train. 
The following figures provide an example of front and flank collisions. A route protects a zone from one 
signal to another signal. The URBALIS™ / U400 (CBTC) system ensures that "Route A" and "Route B" are

3.1.2.1.2 Perform route cancellation 
A zone upstream of the signal protecting a route is defined and called “Approach Locking Section” (ALS). 
On reception of a route cancellation request from ATS operator (from ATS work -station), the URBALIS™ / 
U400 (CBTC) system immediately sets the route origin signal to restrictive state. 
Once a route has been set and locked and no train is in approach locking section, a route cancellation control 
from operator releases the route immediately; a route with the same origin signal can be set immediately. 
Once a train is in approach locking section of a signal origin of route and this signal is or has been controlled 
permissive in field or toward ATC, a route cancellation control from operator releases the route only: 
 After a timer, called Route Release Timer proving the train is stopped. 
 Or as soon as the trackside ATC guarantees to the IXL that the train will stop before the restrictive 
signal ahead.
